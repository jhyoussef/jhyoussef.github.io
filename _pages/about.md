Hello! My name is Julia, and I am a recent graduate from the University of Chicago, currently based in Hyde Park. Broadly, I am interested in reconstructing paleoecologies through time, using quantitative and comparative approaches to examine organisms as parts of larger ecological and evolutionary systems. Alongside paleontological research, I am also developing a background in scientific illustration and paleoart.

Interested in paleontology and planetary science, I majored in Geophysical Sciences with a minor in Astrophysics. I tend to understand Earth history as a dynamic biogeochemical system shaped by evolution, extinction, and origination across deep time. Much of my current work grows out of that perspective, combining computational and creative approaches to better understand how organisms and ecosystems changed, and how that reconstruction can be done from incomplete records and inherited frameworks.

My recent research has focused on pterosaur phylogeny, mammalian herbivory, and methodological questions around ecological and evolutionary inference. Using approaches ranging from Bayesian phylogenetics to multivariate ecological modeling, I am particularly interested in convergence, long-term vertebrate evolutionary patterns, ecosystem recoveries, and the relationship between the reality of the past and the systems we use to represent it.

Alongside research, I use writing and visual art to explore paleontology and reconstruction from a more personal and interpretive perspective. I am interested in how scientific models, images, and narratives shape our understanding of past and present life, and humanity's position within it. My artwork is both a means of improving my understanding through anatomy, research, and reconstruction, and a way to bring a long-gone past back into view.

Outside of my research and art, I am interested in the philosophy of science, visualizing the past, and religious syncretism in antiquity. I also enjoy gardening, rock climbing, and concerts.
