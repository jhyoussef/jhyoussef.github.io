# Welcome!

Hi! AS I've said in the "about me" post, my name is Julia, a semi-recent graduate from the University of Chicago. I was a geoscience major interested in paleontology, planetary science, and earth history, who also spent time on art and an amateur interest in religious history. 

This site exists for two primary reasons: primarily, as an academic website, but also, to host my writing and other science-related artwork. By recording academic topics outsite the context of papers, reports, or presentations, I hope to deepen my understanding of my areas of interest. And, in hosting a website, I hope to provide a respository of consolidated work (or data, or figures) for both mine and my fellow researchers use. In my writing, I plan to discuss recent publications, artwork, current events, and my owb experiences as a first-gen-low-income woman in academia. 

Inspired by Astrobites, 7DaysofScience, and the many WordPress paleoartist sites (Mark Witton, David Hone, etc.) I've been following for years, this blog may host anything from musings on the way we represent evolution, to entries on UChicago specifically, to short responses to pop-science works (looking at you, "Life on Our Planet"). Generally, grappling with the role paleontology has in our daily lives, society, and my own academic trajectory. If you're interested in bigger questions – how we define evolutionary "success," where we fit on the tree of life, and how we feel about those relationships – that will likely be here. If you want to read my grievances about obscure papers or fawn over the newest discoveries on niche clades (with a special fondness for _Nyctosauridae_ and _Anurognathidae_, both pterosaurs, and some mammalian lineages), that can also be found. 

But, perhaps most importantly, this website represents a return-to-self. It is a place to articulate why I am doing paleontology, why academia, why this path (in this economy????). Its a place to practice my writing, practice my art, and documentation of any progress I make. 

Welcome!
